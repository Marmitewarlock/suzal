A ruddy skinned peasant woman inspects a vast wheat field stretching out in front of her; she walks with a cane across the rough ground through the headland into the heart of the crop. Standing with a slight twist she winces and puts a hand to her lower back. The skies are grey and cloudy but the cracked muddy earth shows the weeks of sun that beat down onto the farmland previously. It's still humid even though the morning is dark and the woman wipes sweat from her brow. She takes a step closer to the nearest plant and peers closely at the ear, gently teasing out a few wheat grains.

"They're a bit small but maybe they'll grow."

Standing back up something catches her eye. Tall and leaning ears of rye are peeking over the crop, their distinctive thin ears speckled with black growths. Visions of men going insane and entire flour stores wasted flash across the back of her mind.

"Fucking rye."

— says a pasty skinned working‑class woman as she inspects a sample of rye seed. She's leaning over a glass worktop with a metal spatula in one hand, sifting through a pile of seed. She pushes some seeds before her, inspects them and pulls a few out, dropping them into a small dish.

"At least when it's ergot the colour sorter can get it out."

Every year I have this same feeling — that I'm living experiences that so many before me have done. Complaining about the quality of a crop, worrying about the harvest. I may not be out in the fields but I've somehow ended up in this timeless agricultural world. I know it's not the only work where you get this feeling but during my second harvest I remember making bread in my kitchen and as I was kneading bread I was looking out the window and worrying about how the heavy rain was going to affect the harvest. Suddenly I was wearing linen, complicated underwear and without my glasses I was seeing two balls of dough. And the balls were blurry.

It's an early harvest this year. Generally a good thing? At least I think so. The sooner the crops are out of the field the sooner the lab I work in can do our magic. It's a slow build initially and then suddenly we're in the lab at 6am and leaving when it's dark. I think I look forward to it every year — the excitement and getting ready to get stuck in. And then a month or so goes by and I start to go just a little bit insane.

This year feels like I'm going in a little blind. From the seed point of view I have more experience and knowledge every harvest and I can put that to good use but personally? I'm hot off the most difficult nine months of my life, my body feels stiff and weak, my energy levels aren't up to snuff and this week I've got a bloody cold!!

The peasant girl working the field wouldn't have been able to get that weird lump checked out, it wouldn't have been bombarded with radiation or skilfully sliced out. She wouldn't have had the luxury of months and months of skilled nursing and time off work to heal. She'd have a weird growing lump and then eventually, a bad cough. Then it would get worse.

Every year I think of her now but this year especially
