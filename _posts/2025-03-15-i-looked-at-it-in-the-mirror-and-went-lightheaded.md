I’m no stranger to going lightheaded when I think too much about the pound of flesh that got scooped out of my back, but it’s been a while since I last went all Victorian gentlewoman, so I was a little surprised when I became a bit woozy after checking out my back hole in the mirror.

For those not in the know, I had surgery to remove a 13cm wide sarcoma from my lower back in February and it’s been a long and arduous journey as I’ve been healing. The nurses at my GP surgery were delighted to tell me I wouldn’t need to come in again after last week, and when speaking on the phone with one of them today I was told “I hope I never see you again.” Which was genuinely lovely.

So this evening I got in the shower and for the final time whipped off the little dressing. At this point it was essentially a fancy plaster but kinder to my skin and didn’t make me swear when I pulled it off. Removing it myself was very strange initially — having to reach back and touch near the wound was something I’ve not exactly been eager to do. I would poke and prod a bit when I had the vac dressing on, but that felt so secure that it wasn’t unpleasant. But pulling a dressing and packing off? Oh it was gross and strange and interesting. Tonight though? No healing gunk (shout out to healing gunk, you’re the best). Just a clean plaster with wrinkled edges telling me “I’ve done my job now”.

I tottered to my room wrapped in my towel singing “I’m freeeeeeeeee free holing” and plonked myself on my bed, giddy with my naked little back hole. At this point over the last month or so I’d dry off, air out my pal, get dressed and then ask my wonderfully obliging housemate to patch up my back hole, attend to my butt crack, pop on a temporary dressing before I head to the GP. Instead I got my jammies on and had a gander in the mirror.

My back is not the shape it was before. Just above my butt crack is a considerable indent leading to a circular scar reminiscent of a trolley token slot. On each side of the scar is what I’ve been calling a pleat of skin, similar and yet distinctly different from my usual lovely back rolls. The scar itself is a little pink and a little dry but doesn’t look sore at all; it has a tiny leftover scab which is very much ready for retirement.

It’s weird. But it’s more symmetrical than I realised and even though I needed a sit down after briefly examining it, I found myself not disliking it. I have a strangely shaped back now. It was strange before with the lump which showed off to the right-hand side like I’d hidden a creme egg underneath my skin.

I prefer it this way.
