I've always had a kind of tense relationship with fitness. This is what happens when you grow up with giant primary school tits and as soon as you show interest in a sport early 2000s PE teachers are ready to swoop in and say no, you're not allowed. Why can't we do cricket Joanne*?? Or hockey? Or rugby?? Oh but we can go do cross country running with no training and a pair of fucking bazongas??

Thanks, Joanne, now there's a load of millennial women only finding out what exercise they enjoy in their 30s.

The summer before my diagnosis I was finally getting into a rhythm with yoga, swimming and lifting. Hell, I even joined a cricket club!! Then harvest arrived and all over harvest's coattails was my diagnosis. I spent winter in Nottingham with no chance to do yoga or swim and then the NHS stole part of my lower back in February. Now I'm trying to rebuild. I was still attached to my vacuum dressing when I got back to doing yoga (Meg's Yin/Nidra sessions were the highlight of my week), that was how much I wanted it. I adore yoga but don't mistake that for being good at it. Back when I was practising with the wonderful Beth I eventually got to a point where I was almost almost happy with my downward dog.

In the background are three white singing bowls, a green yoga mat, and a person seated on the floor with a patterned cushion.](#)

*Strawberry moon yoga with Meg. Probably one of my all time favourite sessions.*

Now I'm going to classes at my local gym which, for a gym, are really good. I'm very picky when it comes to yoga. Utterly spoiled by Beth and Meg. But Sophie, the lass who does these classes, is fantastic. The right balance of chill and fitness and she doesn't automatically assume everyone can tie themselves up into a pretzel. That didn't stop me from having a right old cry in my last session.

I think there's a certain part of my recovery where I've been blanking out quite how tight and unyielding my body is. It's better than March which is great, but my brain forgets what it was like in September. And then I try to do a simple forward bend over my leg and it seems like my foot has travelled several miles away whilst I wasn't looking. Normally it's just my belly getting in the way, that's fine but I'm not even reaching the point where she's in the way at the moment.

On Tuesday after yoga I cried in my car in the gym car park and booked a physio. My NHS physios have been amazing but right now I'm going to make the most of having work health perks because Pete and Jo don't need me constantly bothering them.

How can I treat myself more gently than this?

A few weeks ago I went to YHA Ravenstor in the Peak District for a bit of R&R and it was indeed lovely save for the weather approaching 30 and the location of the dorm being on the third floor of a ~120 year old building.

![A large stone house with steep roofs, multiple chimneys, and bay windows, set on a raised terrace with steps leading to a grassy garden. Picnic tables and benches are scattered below, surrounded by shrubs and trees under a clear blue sky.](#)

*The garden I spent my weekend lounging in.*

I lounged on the ground on a little picnic blanket switching between my crochet and my book for most of that weekend. I felt like a rich Victorian woman who has taken herself off to a sanatorium. The tiny pastries and beautiful scenery my medicine. Around me were some lovely youth hostel stereotypes. Families and friends with great legs and packed lunches. A mountaineering club of charming young posh students. Solo travellers armed with backpacks and books.
