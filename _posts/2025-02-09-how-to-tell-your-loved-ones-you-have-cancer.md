Telling my closest friends and family that the random lump on my back was cancerous varied from sobbing on the phone to my mom to dropping highly edited messages in a group chat.

I wanted to say “Guess who's got caaanccceerrr” but it never happened. In the end it was lots of softening of the word. At the time the word itself, “cancer”, seemed too big, too scary. It didn't feel like it fit with the positive attitude both myself and my medical team had. It was still there though and subconsciously I avoided it, spoke about my sarcoma, my lump. It was cancerous, not Cancer.

I did tell one friend through the medium of jazz hands though.

Throughout my radiotherapy treatment all of my close friends knew and I kept folk updated through Instagram stories. Mostly photos of the radiotherapy department and snippets of me pottering around Nottingham City Hospital. My family knew and extended family got frequent reports from my Mom, Grandad and Aunt. My extended friends though? How do you tell someone who you see every few months about this without dropping a bombshell? It was hard enough telling all of my close friends. The same conversation again and again was so very tiring. Time and time again I delegated the task. Pulled the cancer card to get out of the conversation.

Then I was coming to the end of my radiotherapy and soon enough I'd be off work for a few months and have a big old dent in my back. I wanted people to know what I was going through. Damn I wanted the well wishes.

So I dropped a 2700‑word blog post, closed my laptop and proceeded to wander around the house in circles whilst everyone in my life read about my cancer, my treatment and far too much about my pubes. And honestly, I find it was the best decision I made. Getting it all into words was a slog. I wrote most of it sat in a local cafe and found it hard not to tear up on occasion. This was the first time I'd confronted everything as a whole and told the story. And damn, it was cathartic. I spent the rest of the weekend on a high, full of love, support and ego. Laughter is a pretty good medicine but writing — that's a therapy I can't praise highly enough.

I'm starting this blog to continue my lyrical therapy. Not as a writing exercise like I treat my normal blog but as a release, a way to talk through my experiences with myself and hopefully learn from others' experiences.

If you want to read my long‑form pieces I wrote *Lump* as I was finishing my radiotherapy and *Hole* towards the ~end of my post‑surgery healing. I think I have a third piece in me but whilst I still have a grotty little scab on my back I don't think it's ready.

So hello, and welcome to Sarcoma Sunday where I'll be talking far too much about my hole.
